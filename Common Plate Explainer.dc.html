Where we sit in the food-tech market

Everyone is measuring food. Almost no one is measuring how it’s experienced.

The funded players cluster into three categories — waste analytics, surplus redistribution, and generic feedback tools. They compete on cost saved or meals moved. None of them own the quality-and-dignity layer for community food. That gap is the opening.

3 · Generic feedback tools The default today — and the status quo we’re actually replacing

Most providers today reach for SurveyMonkey, Google Forms, Typeform — or paper comment cards. Cheap and universal, but not built for food, not anonymous by design, and they produce raw responses, not aggregated intelligence.

For us: this is the real incumbent. We win by being purpose-built — food-specific metrics, anonymous by default, toxicity-screened, and benchmarked across sites instead of one-off surveys.

Where Common Plate wins

We’re the only evidence layer pointed at the human experience of community food.

Open quadrant

No funded player measures experience & quality for mission orgs. We own it.

Software, not hardware

No cameras or scales to install — deployable in weeks across many small sites.

Evidence for funding

Our reports become the impact evidence partners need to win grants — sticky by design.

Partner, don’t fight

Olio & FareShare are channels, not rivals — we sit on top of their logistics.

Strategic readTHREE MOVES
1
Don’t out-fund — out-focus

Olio raised $43M chasing logistics. We don’t need that scale of capital; we need depth in one category nobody else serves.

2
Borrow the proven motion

Leanpath’s “tech + coaching” and Winnow’s “data drives behaviour” both work — apply them to quality instead of cost.

3
Wedge against survey tools

Our true competitor is the comment card and the generic form. Beat them on food-specific, anonymous, benchmarked intelligence.
